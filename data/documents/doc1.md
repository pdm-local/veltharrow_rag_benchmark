Veltharrow Municipal Charter and Governance Reference
Consolidated Edition, 2024

**Preamble**

Whereas the municipal community now known as Veltharrow was first settled in Spring 1841 as a seasonal lumber camp with no permanent residents;

Whereas permanent settlement was recorded on March 3, 1844, when the first permanent dwelling, the Vanthorpe family cabin, was recorded in the Gorrath Ridge area;

Whereas official incorporation occurred on September 14, 1857, under the authority of the Orvaine Province Territorial Council, Resolution No. 31-1857;

Whereas the original municipal name at incorporation was Veltharrow Mill Settlement;

Whereas the municipal name was shortened by Ordinance of Consolidation No. 4 on June 2, 1883, to Veltharrow;

Whereas the First Charter was granted on September 14, 1857;

Whereas the First Charter Revision occurred on April 7, 1901, and expanded the ward system;

Whereas the Second Charter Revision occurred on November 19, 1948, and constituted post-war administrative reform;

Whereas the Third Charter Revision occurred on February 28, 1974, and established the current Second-Class Charter status;

Therefore, this Consolidated Edition, 2024, sets forth the identity, geography, governance structure, officers, councillors, advisory bodies, ward mapping, and census reference for the Municipality of Veltharrow.

### Section 3. Ordinance R-2014-007

The full name is **Veltharrow Residential Density Bonus Ordinance of 2014**.

The adoption date is **June 17, 2014**.

The effective date is **August 1, 2014**.

The Council vote was **8–1 in favor**.

The subject was the affordable housing income threshold used to qualify for density bonuses in Zone R-2.

The old value, pre-August 2014, was that affordable units qualified if rents/prices were affordable to households earning **≤ 50%** of municipal median household income.

The new value, effective **August 1, 2014**, current, is that affordable units qualify if rents/prices are affordable to households earning **≤ 60%** of municipal median household income.

The new value is **≤ $35,246** based on the **2019 census** figure of **$58,743**.

The sponsor was **Councillor Emmelyn Foscroft**.

Emmelyn Foscroft represents **Drasswell Ward, Seat 4B**.

### Section 4. Ordinance Z-2017-009

The full name is **Veltharrow Comprehensive Zoning Amendment Ordinance of 2017**.

The adoption date is **October 3, 2017**.

The effective date is **January 1, 2018**.

The Council vote was **5–4 in favor**.

The vote was the narrowest margin of any included ordinance.

The subject was an omnibus zoning amendment.

For Gorrath Ridge Maximum Building Height, the old value was maximum building height in R-1 zone for Gorrath Ridge parcels above **350 m** elevation equal to **9.5 meters**, the same as standard R-1.

For Gorrath Ridge Maximum Building Height, the new value was maximum building height for Gorrath Ridge parcels above **350 m** elevation equal to **8.1 meters** under the Gorrath Ridgeline View Protection Policy.

For Lake Orvaine Reach Shoreline Setback, the old value was shoreline setback in the then-unoverlaid residential zone equal to **25.0 meters** from ordinary high-water mark.

For Lake Orvaine Reach Shoreline Setback, the new value was shoreline setback in Zone PO-1 equal to **38.5 meters** from ordinary high-water mark.

For Pellgate Flats Height Overlay, the old value was maximum building height in R-3, Pellgate Flats, equal to **28.0 meters**, the same as standard R-3.

For Pellgate Flats Height Overlay, the new value was maximum building height in R-3, Pellgate Flats, under Pellgate Special Height Overlay, equal to **21.0 meters**.

For Pellgate Flats Height Overlay, the new value was **6 stories**.

The stated reason was aircraft approach path proximity.

The dissenting votes were **Councillors Rund Pallister, Prinna Valcourt, Marthyl Dunnegan, Oresten Bluffe**.

### Section 5. Ordinance C-2005-001

The full name is **Cornrath Central Ground-Floor Commercial Activation Ordinance of 2005**.

The adoption date is **February 15, 2005**.

The effective date is **July 1, 2005**.

The Council vote was **9–0**.

The Council vote was unanimous.

The subject was mandatory ground-floor commercial use along Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road in Zone C-3.

The old value, pre-July 2005, was no mandatory ground-floor use requirement.

The old value permitted residential and commercial uses on ground floors in Zone C-3 without restriction.

The new value, effective **July 1, 2005**, was that **100%** of ground-floor street frontage on Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road must be active commercial, dining, or civic use.

The new value prohibited residential units at ground floor on these frontages.

The grandfathering cutoff date was **December 31, 2004**.

Residential ground-floor uses legally established as of **December 31, 2004** are legal nonconforming uses.

The sponsor was **Mayor Colwick Durrath**.

Colwick Durrath was predecessor to Mayor Ovenspoor.

Colwick Durrath served **2002–2010**.

The effective date is **January 1, 2022**.

The Council vote was **6–3 in favor**.

### Section 7. Ordinance SP-2019-001

The full name is **Drasswell Common Flood Proofing Certification Program Ordinance of 2019**.

The adoption date is **April 30, 2019**.

The effective date is **September 1, 2019**.

The Council vote was **7–2 in favor**.

The subject was the Drasswell Common Flood Proofing Certification Program and the renewal period for flood proofing certificates.

The old value, pre-September 2019, was that Flood Proofing Certificates under predecessor administrative policy were renewed every **5 years**.

The new value, effective **September 1, 2019**, current, is that Flood Proofing Certificates are renewed every **7 years**.

The administering office is the **Director of Public Works**.

The Director of Public Works is **Cael Drossmund**.

The minimum base flood elevation standard codified by the ordinance is lowest habitable floor equal to base flood elevation **97.4 m AMSL** plus **0.8 m**, equal to **98.2 m AMSL**.

The prior standard under the **2012** administrative policy was base flood elevation plus **0.5 m**, equal to **97.9 m AMSL**.
