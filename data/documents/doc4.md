Municipality of Veltharrow
Second-Class Charter Municipality
Republic of Drenmoor

Province of Orvaine
Municipal Seat: Veltharrow Civic Hall, 14 Cornrath Square, Veltharrow

This Compendium records the canonical ordinance amendments of the Municipality of Veltharrow for the period 2005–2021. It is prepared as a formal statement of amendment history, superseded values, current values, effective dates, council votes, named sponsors or administering officers where given, and operative status as of the FY2024 reference. Each entry below identifies the old value that was superseded and the new value established by the ordinance. Where an ordinance contains more than one measurable sub-change, each sub-change is stated separately.

## Ordinance C-2005-001

**Full Name:** Cornrath Central Ground-Floor Commercial Activation Ordinance of 2005.

**Adoption Date:** February 15, 2005.

**Effective Date:** July 1, 2005.

**Council Vote:** 9–0 (unanimous).

**Sponsor:** Mayor Colwick Durrath, predecessor to Mayor Ovenspoor, served 2002–2010.

**Subject:** Established mandatory ground-floor commercial use along Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road in Zone C-3.

Ordinance C-2005-001 amended the land-use rules applicable to Zone C-3, the Cornrath Central Business District. The subject frontage is the ground-floor street frontage on Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road in Zone C-3. The ordinance created a mandatory active-use requirement for those frontages and displaced the prior condition under which residential and commercial ground-floor uses were permitted without the restriction stated below.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance C-2005-001:** Prior to July 1, 2005, there was no mandatory ground-floor use requirement; residential and commercial uses were permitted on ground floors in Zone C-3 without restriction.

**NEW Value, effective July 1, 2005:** 100% of ground-floor street frontage on Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road must be active commercial, dining, or civic use. Residential units are prohibited at ground floor on these frontages.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative value is the new value effective July 1, 2005: 100% of ground-floor street frontage on Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road must be active commercial, dining, or civic use, and residential units are prohibited at ground floor on these frontages.

**Grandfathering Cutoff Date:** Residential ground-floor uses legally established as of December 31, 2004 are legal nonconforming uses.

The C-3 rule reflected in the current zoning code states that 100% of ground-floor street frontage on Cornrath Avenue and Drasswell Road must be occupied by active commercial, dining, or civic uses. No residential units, parking entrances, or blank walls are permitted on these frontages at ground level. Lobby entrances for upper-floor office or residential uses may occupy up to 12% of total street frontage width. A property with a legally established residential ground-floor use as of December 31, 2004, prior to Ordinance C-2005-001 coming into effect, may continue as a legal nonconforming use but may not expand the residential footprint.

## Ordinance T-2007-001

**Full Name:** Veltharrow Property Assessment Standardization Ordinance of 2007.

**Adoption Date:** March 6, 2007.

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2008.

**Council Vote:** 7–2 in favor.

**Administering Officer at Time of Passage:** Chief Assessor Dolvane Prescroft, predecessor to Brixton Hallvane.

**Subject:** Changed the assessment ratio, meaning assessed value as a percentage of fair market value, used for property tax calculations.

Ordinance T-2007-001 amended the property tax assessment ratio applicable to the computation of assessed value from fair market value. The ordinance was an interim revision. It expressly replaced the pre-2008 assessment ratio with a revised assessment ratio effective January 1, 2008. The ordinance is also cross-referenced by the later assessment-ratio amendment adopted as Ordinance T-2009-003.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance T-2007-001:** Pre-2008 assessment ratio = 65% of fair market value.

**NEW Value, effective January 1, 2008 under Ordinance T-2007-001:** Assessment ratio = 71% of fair market value.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The 71% assessment ratio established by Ordinance T-2007-001 is not the current operative FY2024 assessment ratio. It was superseded by Ordinance T-2009-003. The current operative FY2024 assessment ratio is 73% of fair market value, as established by Ordinance T-2009-003 and referenced in the FY2024 property tax provisions.

The property tax assessment-ratio history for this ordinance is therefore stated in full as follows: the old value before the effective date of Ordinance T-2007-001 was 65% of fair market value; the new value created by Ordinance T-2007-001, effective January 1, 2008, was 71% of fair market value; and the ordinance itself was identified as an interim revision subject to the subsequent further change made by Ordinance T-2009-003.

## Ordinance T-2009-003

**Full Name:** Veltharrow Assessment Ratio Adjustment Ordinance of 2009.

**Adoption Date:** September 22, 2009.

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2010.

**Council Vote:** 6–3 in favor.

**Subject:** Second revision to the assessment ratio for property tax calculation.

Ordinance T-2009-003 amended the assessment ratio previously established by Ordinance T-2007-001. The assessment ratio is the percentage of fair market value used to determine assessed value for property tax calculations. The ordinance replaced the 71% value that had been effective January 1, 2008 under Ordinance T-2007-001 with the 73% value effective January 1, 2010.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance T-2009-003:** Assessment ratio = 71% of fair market value, effective January 1, 2008 under Ordinance T-2007-001.

**NEW Value, effective January 1, 2010:** Assessment ratio = 73% of fair market value.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative assessment ratio as of FY2024 is 73% of fair market value.

The FY2024 property tax provisions state that all rates are expressed as mills per $1,000 of assessed value, and that assessed value = 73% of fair market value, established by Ordinance T-2009-003. The FY2024 property tax provisions also state an assessment ratio of 73.0%. The general municipal levy rate for FY2024 is 14.73 mills, applied municipally-wide. The current operative assessment ratio used with those property tax provisions is the 73% value established by this ordinance.

For completeness, the supersession sequence is stated separately: before Ordinance T-2007-001, the assessment ratio was 65% of fair market value; under Ordinance T-2007-001, effective January 1, 2008, the assessment ratio was 71% of fair market value; under Ordinance T-2009-003, effective January 1, 2010, the assessment ratio became 73% of fair market value; and as of the FY2024 reference the operative assessment ratio remains 73% of fair market value.

## Ordinance R-2014-007

**Full Name:** Veltharrow Residential Density Bonus Ordinance of 2014.

**Adoption Date:** June 17, 2014.

**Effective Date:** August 1, 2014.

**Council Vote:** 8–1 in favor.

**Sponsor:** Councillor Emmelyn Foscroft, Drasswell Ward, Seat 4B.

**Subject:** Changed the affordable housing income threshold used to qualify for density bonuses in Zone R-2.

Ordinance R-2014-007 amended the affordable housing income threshold used in Zone R-2 for density-bonus qualification. The ordinance did not replace the full text of all R-2 development standards; it changed the qualifying income threshold for affordable units used to determine eligibility for the density-bonus provision. The current Zone R-2 density provision states that the standard maximum density is 24 dwelling units per hectare of net lot area. It further states that projects that include at least 15% affordable units may develop at up to 31 dwelling units per hectare if the affordability condition is satisfied.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance R-2014-007:** Before August 1, 2014, affordable units qualified if rents or prices were affordable to households earning ≤ 50% of municipal median household income.

**NEW Value, effective August 1, 2014:** Affordable units qualify if rents or prices are affordable to households earning ≤ 60% of municipal median household income.

**Canonical Computed Value Stated in the Ordinance Record:** The new value is also stated as ≤ $35,246 based on the 2019 census figure of $58,743.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative affordable housing income threshold as of FY2024 is the new value effective August 1, 2014: affordable units qualify if rents or prices are affordable to households earning ≤ 60% of municipal median household income, stated in the canonical record as ≤ $35,246 based on the 2019 census figure of $58,743.

The R-2 rule associated with this ordinance is Rule R-2.1, Maximum Density. The standard under Rule R-2.1 is a maximum of 24 dwelling units per hectare of net lot area. Exception R-2.1(a) provides that projects that include at least 15% affordable units, defined as affordable to households earning ≤ 60% of municipal median household income, i.e., ≤ $35,246 based on 2019 census, may develop at up to 31 dwelling units per hectare. Exception R-2.1(b) separately provides that senior housing developments, where ≥ 80% of occupants are age 65 or older, may develop at up to 34 dwelling units per hectare.

The old and new values for the amendment are not to be merged. The pre-August 2014 value was ≤ 50% of municipal median household income. The value effective August 1, 2014 and operative as of FY2024 is ≤ 60% of municipal median household income, with the canonical stated dollar expression of ≤ $35,246 based on the 2019 census figure of $58,743.

## Ordinance Z-2017-009

**Full Name:** Veltharrow Comprehensive Zoning Amendment Ordinance of 2017.

**Adoption Date:** October 3, 2017.

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2018.

**Council Vote:** 5–4 in favor.

**Dissenting Votes:** Councillors Rund Pallister, Prinna Valcourt, Marthyl Dunnegan, Oresten Bluffe.

**Subject:** Omnibus zoning amendment; three specific measurable changes are called out: Gorrath Ridge Maximum Building Height, Lake Orvaine Reach Shoreline Setback, and Pellgate Flats Height Overlay.

Ordinance Z-2017-009 is the last comprehensive amendment to the Veltharrow Comprehensive Zoning Code referenced in the canonical record. The governing code is the Veltharrow Comprehensive Zoning Code, enacted by Ordinance Z-1987-001, effective January 1, 1988, and last comprehensively amended by Ordinance Z-2017-009. The zoning map reference is Municipal Zoning Map VTH-ZM-2023, on file with the Director of Planning.

### Z-2017-009 Sub-Change A: Gorrath Ridge Maximum Building Height

**Subject of Sub-Change A:** Maximum building height in the R-1 zone for Gorrath Ridge parcels above 350 meters elevation.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance Z-2017-009:** Maximum building height in R-1 zone for Gorrath Ridge parcels above 350 m elevation = 9.5 meters, the same as standard R-1.

**NEW Value, effective January 1, 2018:** Maximum building height for Gorrath Ridge parcels above 350 m elevation = 8.1 meters, under the Gorrath Ridgeline View Protection Policy.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative value for Gorrath Ridge parcels above 350 m elevation is 8.1 meters.

The current R-1 rule states that no structure shall exceed 9.5 meters in height, measured from grade to highest roof point. Exception R-1.2(a) states that accessory structures, including garages and sheds, are limited to 4.2 meters. Exception R-1.2(b) states that structures on Gorrath Ridge parcels above 350 meters elevation are subject to a reduced maximum of 8.1 meters due to the Gorrath Ridgeline View Protection Policy, adopted with Ordinance Z-2017-009.

### Z-2017-009 Sub-Change B: Lake Orvaine Reach Shoreline Setback

**Subject of Sub-Change B:** Shoreline setback applicable to Lake Orvaine Reach, later stated in Zone PO-1, the Lake Orvaine Reach Preservation Overlay.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance Z-2017-009:** Shoreline setback in the then-unoverlaid residential zone = 25.0 meters from ordinary high-water mark.

**NEW Value, effective January 1, 2018:** Shoreline setback in Zone PO-1 = 38.5 meters from ordinary high-water mark.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative shoreline setback in Zone PO-1 is 38.5 meters from the ordinary high-water mark of Lake Orvaine.

The current PO-1 rule states that no structure shall be constructed within 38.5 meters of the ordinary high-water mark of Lake Orvaine. Exception PO-1.1(a) states that water-dependent structures, including docks, boat launches, and pump stations, may be placed within the setback subject to Provincial Environmental Authority permit and Veltharrow Shoreline Development Permit. Exception PO-1.1(b) states that restoration or replacement of legally existing structures within the setback that were damaged by a declared natural disaster may proceed, provided the replacement footprint does not exceed the original footprint.

### Z-2017-009 Sub-Change C: Pellgate Flats Height Overlay

**Subject of Sub-Change C:** Maximum building height in R-3 for Pellgate Flats under the Pellgate Special Height Overlay.

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance Z-2017-009:** Maximum building height in R-3 for Pellgate Flats = 28.0 meters, the same as standard R-3.

**NEW Value, effective January 1, 2018:** Maximum building height in R-3 for Pellgate Flats under the Pellgate Special Height Overlay = 21.0 meters (6 stories), due to aircraft approach path proximity.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative maximum building height for Pellgate Flats under the Pellgate Special Height Overlay is 21.0 meters (6 stories).

The current R-3 rule states that the standard maximum building height is 28.0 meters or 8 stories, whichever is less. Exception R-3.2(a) states that in Pellgate Flats, the Pellgate Special Height Overlay, established by Ordinance Z-2017-009, Section 14.7, limits all structures to 21.0 meters (6 stories) due to aircraft approach path proximity. Exception R-3.2(b) states that rooftop mechanical, stair, and elevator enclosures may extend up to 3.1 meters above the height limit, provided they are set back at least 3.0 meters from all exterior wall planes.

The measurable old and new values created by Ordinance Z-2017-009 are therefore preserved separately. For Gorrath Ridge parcels above 350 m elevation, the old value was 9.5 meters and the new value is 8.1 meters. For Lake Orvaine Reach shoreline setback, the old value was 25.0 meters from ordinary high-water mark and the new value is 38.5 meters from ordinary high-water mark. For Pellgate Flats height, the old value was 28.0 meters and the new value is 21.0 meters (6 stories).

## Ordinance SP-2019-001

**Full Name:** Drasswell Common Flood Proofing Certification Program Ordinance of 2019.

**Adoption Date:** April 30, 2019.

**Effective Date:** September 1, 2019.

**Council Vote:** 7–2 in favor.

**Administering Office:** Director of Public Works, Cael Drossmund.

**Subject:** Established the Drasswell Common Flood Proofing Certification Program and changed the renewal period for flood proofing certificates.

Ordinance SP-2019-001 amended the flood proofing certificate renewal period and codified the minimum base flood elevation standard for relevant Drasswell Common flood proofing rules. The ordinance is reflected in the Zone SP-1 Drasswell Flood Special Purpose provisions and the flood mitigation requirements for adjacent properties.

### SP-2019-001 Certificate Renewal Period

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance SP-2019-001:** Before September 1, 2019, Flood Proofing Certificates under predecessor administrative policy were renewed every 5 years.

**NEW Value, effective September 1, 2019:** Flood Proofing Certificates are renewed every 7 years.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative renewal period for Flood Proofing Certificates is every 7 years.

The current SP-1 rule states that properties that have completed the Drasswell Common Flood Proofing Certification Program, administered under Ordinance SP-2019-001, and hold a valid Flood Proofing Certificate are exempt from per-project elevation review, provided their certificate is current. The certificate is renewable every 7 years.

### SP-2019-001 Minimum Base Flood Elevation Standard

**OLD Value, superseded by Ordinance SP-2019-001:** Under the 2012 administrative policy, the minimum standard had previously been set at base flood elevation + 0.5 m = 97.9 m AMSL.

**NEW Value, codified by Ordinance SP-2019-001:** Lowest habitable floor = base flood elevation (97.4 m AMSL) + 0.8 m = 98.2 m AMSL.

**Current Operative Value as of FY2024:** The current operative minimum habitable floor standard is 98.2 m AMSL, stated as lowest habitable floor = base flood elevation (97.4 m AMSL) + 0.8 m = 98.2 m AMSL.

The current SP-1 rule states that any new construction on properties within 55 meters of a SP-1 zone boundary must elevate the lowest habitable floor to at least 0.8 meters above the 100-year flood elevation as established by the Orvaine Provincial Flood Authority's 2016 Floodplain Mapping. The base flood elevation for Drasswell Common is 97.4 meters AMSL, and the minimum habitable floor is 98.2 meters AMSL.

The exceptions are stated in full. Accessory structures under 14 square meters are exempt from elevation requirements. Properties that have completed the Drasswell Common Flood Proofing Certification Program, administered under Ordinance SP-2019-001, and hold a valid Flood Proofing Certificate are exempt from per-project elevation review, provided their certificate is current and renewable every 7 years.

The old and new values are therefore preserved separately. For certificate renewal, the old value was every 5 years and the new value is every 7 years. For the minimum base flood elevation standard, the old value was base flood elevation + 0.5 m = 97.9 m AMSL under the 2012 administrative policy, and the new value codified by the ordinance is base flood elevation (97.4 m AMSL) + 0.8 m = 98.2 m AMSL.

## Ordinance F-2021-004

**Full Name:** Veltharrow Municipal Sales Tax Rate Adjustment Ordinance of 2021.

**Adoption Date:** November 9, 2021.

**Effective Date:** January 1, 2022.

**Council Vote:** 6–3 in favor.

**Dissenting Votes:** Councillors Prinna Valcourt, Marthyl Dunnegan, Rund Pallister.

**Subject:** Adjusted the municipal sales tax rate to fund the Drasswell Pond Flood Mitigation Capital Program.

Ordinance F-2021-004 amended the municipal sales tax rate and stated a combined rate change while leaving the provincial rate unchanged. It also included a sunset provision for the increase. The ordinance is reflected in the FY2024 fiscal data, which states the municipal sales tax rate as 1.473%, added to the Drenmoor Provincial Sales Tax of 6.000%, with a combined rate of 7.473%.

**OLD Municipal Sales Tax Value, superseded by Ordinance F-2021-004:** Before January 1, 2022, Municipal Sales Tax Rate = 1.218%.

**NEW Municipal Sales Tax Value, effective January 1, 2022:** Municipal Sales Tax Rate = 1.473%.

**Current Operative Municipal Sales Tax Value as of FY2024:** The current operative municipal sales tax rate as of FY2024 is 1.473%.

**Provincial Rate Stated in the Ordinance Record:** Provincial rate unchanged at 6.000%.

**OLD Combined Rate Value, superseded by Ordinance F-2021-004:** Combined rate = 7.218%.

**NEW Combined Rate Value, effective January 1, 2022:** Combined rate = 7.473%.

**Current Operative Combined Rate as of FY2024:** The current operative combined sales tax rate as of FY2024 is 7.473%.

**Sunset Provision:** The 0.255 percentage-point increase is scheduled to expire on December 31, 2029, unless renewed by Council. Base rate of 1.218% remains permanent.

The current FY2024 revenue provisions identify municipal sales tax revenue as $18,447,300 and state the municipal sales tax rate as 1.473%. The same FY2024 fiscal provisions state that the municipal sales tax rate of 1.473% is added to the Drenmoor Provincial Sales Tax of 6.000%, and that the combined rate is 7.473%. The ordinance record separately states that the combined rate increased from 7.218% to 7.473%, that the provincial rate remained 6.000%, that the 0.255 percentage-point increase is scheduled to expire on December 31, 2029 unless renewed by Council, and that the base rate of 1.218% remains permanent.

For purposes of amendment history, the old and new values must be stated without alteration. The pre-January 1, 2022 municipal sales tax rate was 1.218%. The rate effective January 1, 2022 and operative as of FY2024 is 1.473%. The unchanged provincial rate is 6.000%. The old combined rate was 7.218%. The new combined rate is 7.473%. The sunset date for the 0.255 percentage-point increase is December 31, 2029 unless renewed by Council. The permanent base rate stated in the ordinance record is 1.218%.
